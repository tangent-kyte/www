How a job moves

The way a smith works iron, and the way we work software: draw it, shape it, test it in the fire, then keep it sharp.

  1. Drawn up

    A free call, then a written plan: scope, services, timeline and estimate.

  2. Hammered out

    Weekly demos, a shared board, tested code merged every day.

  3. Hardened in the fire

    Load tests, a security review and automated deploys. Monitoring is on before launch.

  4. Kept sharp

    Updates, speed work and new features on a schedule that fits your roadmap.

Questions, answered

Can you take over an existing codebase?

Yes. We start with a short review of the code and the architecture, tell you what we found, then propose a plan to steady it and build on it.

Do I need to know which services I need?

No. Bring the problem or the goal. In the first call we pick the smallest set of services that gets you there, so the work order can go out empty.

Who owns the code and the designs?

You do, from the first commit. Everything lives in your repositories and your cloud accounts, never ours.

How do you price the work?

Sprints get a fixed quote. Dedicated teams and care retainers are billed monthly. You see a written estimate before you commit to anything.

Can you work alongside our developers?

Yes. We join your channels, your board and your code reviews, and we hand the work over to your team whenever you are ready.

What does a normal week look like?

A shared channel for questions, a board you can check at any time, and a demo every week where you see working software, not slides.

What if I only need one small thing?

That is what a sprint is for: one bot, one extension, one audit or one speed pass, done in two to six weeks.
